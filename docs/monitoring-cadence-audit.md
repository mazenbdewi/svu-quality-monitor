# Monitoring cadence audit — 17 November 2026

This is a code and isolated-test audit, not a measurement of the university's production scheduler. Automatic SLO preparation remains disabled by default. No historical observations, monitoring thresholds, SPC formulas, or SLI/SLO formulas were changed.

## Current clock and root cause

`services:check-due` is registered once with `everyMinute()->withoutOverlapping()`; Docker runs `schedule:work`. The command reads every active service and its latest check, regardless of source, and considers it due when `latest.checked_at + check_interval_minutes <= now()`. There is no persisted planned opportunity or `next_check_at`. `checked_at` is the HTTP/API checker **start**, not dispatch, completion, or the nominal cron minute. The default database-queue worker polls with `--sleep=3`; its wait and the HTTP duration are additional to scheduler-poll delay.

If a one-minute service is dispatched at 00:00:00 and starts at 00:00:05, it becomes due at 00:01:05. The 00:01:00 poll is too early; the 00:02:00 poll can dispatch it, already 55 seconds after actual due. A 5-minute service similarly misses 00:05:00 and can be dispatched at 00:06:00. The prior Batch 5C.3 baseline p99 **60.562 s due-to-`Queue::after`** is consistent with this phase alignment: p95 due-to-pre-enqueue marker was 57.560 s, marker-to-worker-start 6.589 s, and worker execution 0.238 s. These are separate percentile distributions and must not be summed. The benchmark used fixed manual poll offsets, not a real `schedule:work` run; its absolute latency is not a production estimate.

Deterministic isolated database-queue tests pin each cron poll to a minute boundary, start the worker five seconds later, and fake the external HTTP response. They verify the queue row's integer-second `created_at`, `Queue::before`, persisted `checked_at`, and `Queue::after`. In this controlled phase all four timestamps for the first check are respectively 00:00:00, 00:00:00, 00:00:05, and 00:00:05. The equal start/end clock values are a **simulated instant completion**, not a measured zero-duration network request. Results:

| Configured interval | Actual dispatch minutes | Persisted starts | Consecutive start gaps | Actual due times after first start |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 min | 00, 02, 04 | 00:00:05, 00:02:05, 00:04:05 | 2, 2 min | 00:01:05, 00:03:05 |
| 5 min | 00, 06, 12 | 00:00:05, 00:06:05, 00:12:05 | 6, 6 min | 00:05:05, 00:11:05 |
| 15 min | 00, 16, 32 | 00:00:05, 00:16:05, 00:32:05 | 16, 16 min | 00:15:05, 00:31:05 |

These are possible, reproducible gaps, not a claim that every real service always has an interval-plus-one-minute gap. Varying worker delay, missed scheduler polls, failures, and the next check's execution time can change the gap. A manual check can also defer an automatic one because the latest-check relation has no `source=automatic` filter. Repeated polls before a worker runs formerly queued duplicate check jobs: direct `Dispatcher::dispatch()` bypassed the `ShouldBeUnique` acquisition performed by Laravel's pending-dispatch path. The command now acquires the job's unique cache lock before dispatch, counts pending duplicates separately, and releases the lock if dispatch throws. The worker's existing unique-job completion path releases it on completion. This is a duplicate-prevention correction, **not** a cadence-policy change. Its 120-second lease and shared atomic cache remain operational prerequisites; a backlog beyond the lease is not proved duplicate-free.

## Scientific interpretation

Executed-check availability is still successful eligible checks divided by all **executed** eligible checks; missing opportunities are not failures or successes in that denominator. Thus a sparse all-success sample can show 100% executed-check availability while coverage is inadequate. In the controlled one-minute six-minute window, coverage reported **3 covered of 6 expected slots (50%)**; the three successful checks do not establish one-minute monitoring. Coverage calculators use configured-interval slots and current configuration, not a durable log of exact scheduler opportunities. For larger intervals, phase drift can be masked temporarily by coarse slots; slot coverage is not proof of dispatch deadline compliance. Neither an adaptive-due 60/60 completion count nor a high check-success ratio proves fixed-frequency sampling.

Hourly P-chart subgroup sizes use the checks actually observed (`n`), while expected/covered counts and warnings use configured cadence. Reduced or load-correlated sampling can change `n`, precision and missing-coverage status; I/MR point spacing and Phase II signal timeliness can also change. Reliability reporting may retain descriptive success ratios but withholds verified availability when coverage is insufficient. The 28-day SLO calibration coverage gate can reject sparse data, but a passing aggregate coverage percentage does not validate exact cadence. Frozen Phase I references and past Phase II results must not be rewritten. Any future cadence change needs an explicitly recorded effective **UTC cutover instant** (with local timezone and policy version), separate pre/post cohorts, and new comparability review even if `check_interval_minutes` itself stays the same; existing configuration fingerprints do not by themselves encode scheduler-policy changes.

## Acceptance decision required before deployment

The earlier Batch 5C.1 **proposed** p95 actual-due-to-dispatch <=5 s and p99 actual-due-to-completion <=50 s were not formally superseded. The later 65/105-second bounds were frozen for a local adaptive-due benchmark and passed there, but that does not authorize replacing the earlier production proposal. Under minute polling with a due time just after a poll, the earlier dispatch bound is structurally unachievable without changing the scheduling semantics or poll frequency. The local baseline also exceeded the 50-second completion bound. Neither bound is a Google/ISO standard.

An operator/research owner must first choose and document whether the configured interval means (a) a fixed nominal sampling cadence or (b) a minimum elapsed time since the previous check start. If (a), the smallest no-new-queue candidate is to align eligibility to the preceding **calendar minute slot**, rather than `checked_at`'s seconds, and keep the existing unique-job/overlap safeguards. That candidate still needs explicit tests for long checks, late workers, manual checks, missed polls, maintenance, and a cutover record; it is **not** implemented here and is not a complete durable opportunity ledger. If (b), retain the algorithm but label the setting and coverage interpretation honestly and prospectively approve bounds that include up to one minute of poll latency. Neither choice justifies retroactively changing historical measurements or relaxing a gate after seeing outcomes.

For prospective acceptance, record separately: configured frequency and consecutive persisted check-start gaps; actual due-to-successful-enqueue lateness; enqueue-to-`Queue::before` wait; `Queue::before`-to-`Queue::after` execution; and due-to-persisted-result end-to-end lateness. Count every due opportunity exactly once at a fixed cutoff as completed success, completed failure, running, queued, or due/not-dispatched; list not-yet-due separately. Agree on p95/p99 limits and zero uninvestigated duplicates, missed opportunities and failed jobs **before** a representative run. A full cron-minute `schedule:work` trial, sustained load, shared-cache topology, actual production service mix, nighttime job overlap, and production worker/database capacity remain unverified. This audit does not authorize code-only deployment, automatic SLO preparation, or Pilot activation.
